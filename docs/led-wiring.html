Build A · with level shifter — recommended

The reliable build. The 74AHCT125 converts the Pi's 3.3 V into a clean 5 V data signal — worth it right next to the pumps and relays.

Build B · direct, no level shifter

The simplest build. Data runs straight from GPIO21 (pin 40) to the strip at 3.3 V. Try this first; if the first LEDs flicker or show wrong colors, switch to Build A.

Same as the connection checklist below, minus the three 74AHCT125 rows (VCC, 1OE, and the pin 3 output). Keep the 470 Ω resistor — it protects the first LED — and keep the data lead short and away from the relay/pump wiring.

Physical pin numbers, board oriented with the header on the right. This 40-pin layout is identical across Pi 2, 3, 3B+, 4 and 5. This build uses pin 2 (5V) · pin 6 (GND) · pin 40 (GPIO21, data). GPIO21 drives the strip over the PCM peripheral, so the Pi's onboard audio keeps working. GPIO18 / pin 12 is the PWM alternate but conflicts with that audio; pin 4 and the other seven grounds are spare.

Why the 74AHCT125 level shifter?

The signal is out of spec without it

A WS2812 counts its data line as a "1" only above 0.7 × the strip voltage — that's 3.5 V on a 5 V strip. The Pi's GPIO only swings 3.3 V, just under the line. It frequently works anyway, but it's marginal, not guaranteed.

The 74AHCT125 runs on 5 V, happily reads the Pi's 3.3 V as a valid input, and re-drives it as a clean 5 V pulse — a solid, in-spec signal into the first LED.

Why it matters on this machine

Your kiosk switches 16 relays and runs pumps inches from this wiring — a lot of electrical noise. A marginal 3.3 V data line is exactly what that noise turns into flicker and wrong colors, so here the shifter is cheap insurance rather than optional.

Don't want the chip? Either sacrifice the first LED — feed 3.3 V straight into pixel 1 and don't use it, letting its output re-drive a clean 5 V to the rest — or just try 3.3 V direct and add the shifter only if the first LEDs misbehave.

Powering it safely — what causes 90% of failures

  • Common ground. PSU, Pi, level shifter, and strip grounds must all connect. A floating ground is the #1 cause of "does nothing" or random flicker.
  • Size for the combined load. ~3.6 A of LEDs plus ~1.4 A for a Pi 3B+ is ≈ 5 A — so a 5 V / 6 A supply for headroom. An undersized supply sags under an LED surge and browns out the Pi (undervoltage → throttling and SD-card corruption).
  • Star-wire from the PSU. Run a separate +5V/GND pair to the Pi and another to the strip, straight from the PSU terminals — don't chain the Pi's power off the strip's rail. That keeps a big LED current spike from dipping the Pi's voltage. Use adequately thick wire for both.
  • GPIO power bypasses the Pi's protection. Feeding 5 V into pin 2 skips the input fuse and reverse-polarity protection. Triple-check polarity (pin 2 = +5V, pin 6 = GND) — reversed 5 V kills the Pi — and power it this way or over micro-USB, never both at once.
  • Feed the first LED, not the last. Data goes into the DIN end. The arrow on the strip shows flow direction — get it backwards and nothing lights.
Data pin is GPIO21 (pin 40) — it uses the PCM peripheral, so the Pi's onboard audio keeps working; GPIO18 (pin 12) is the PWM alternate but conflicts with audio. Try the strip on 3.3 V data first — add the 74AHCT125 only if the first LEDs glitch.
